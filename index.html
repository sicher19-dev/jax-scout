The Read

Three tough at-bats up top — then the lineup is yours.

Atlanta stacks its real hitters 1-2-3: Baldwin (a leadoff catcher who swings hard on half his hacks), Acuña (the toughest out on the card — 60% hard swings, and he only gets beaten from ahead in the count), and Olson (power, but a 24% strikeout rate the curve can reach). Those three at-bats are about count control and corners — the same plan that produced the easy Soto fly ball in New York.

Then it turns. Dubón hits cleanup with a slow, flat swing and no punch — hard stuff in the zone, two-pitch outs. Albies and Yastrzemski are steep, slow swings that the changeup and curve go right under. Murphy is the strikeout at-bat (misses 35% of the time against your mix). Riley and Harris are boom-or-bust: strikeouts from ahead, trouble from behind. The bottom five of this order is where the strikeouts live.

The two things that decide this one are the two things the New York game taught: strike one (Chicago was 16-of-21 to two strikes; New York was 7-of-17) and the changeup below the knees. Your movement is back — the sinker ran more against the Mets than it did all August. Now it's about where the pitches finish.

THE SHORT VERSION

The way we see it, Atlanta is three real at-bats and six you should win. Baldwin, Acuña and Olson are about the same thing the Soto at-bat was about: corners, count control, and being fine with a walk instead of a mistake. Acuña especially — he swings max-effort more than anyone you've faced except Colson Montgomery, and he's beatable from ahead and dangerous from behind.

The bottom of that order is where the game is. Dubón can't hurt you — two pitches and a ground ball. Albies and Yastrzemski have the steep, slow swings the curve dives under, and the curve is the best pitch you have right now — 64% misses the last three starts. Murphy is the strikeout at-bat every trip. Riley and Harris are the same story as Robert was: get ahead and they strike out, fall behind and it's a problem.

And the two carryovers from New York are the whole outing: strike one to everybody, and the changeup below the knees. Your movement's back — that question's closed. Now it's just where the pitches finish. Whenever the grip photo's handy, we'll pick that conversation up; no rush.
