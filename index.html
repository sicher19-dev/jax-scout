The Read

The softest card of the summer — and the start to get the finishing pitches back.

The A's are playing without their entire core — Kurtz, Rooker and Wilson on the 60-day, Langeliers and Soderstrom on the 10-day — and what's left grades .227 against you, by a wide margin the softest lineup on any card since Chicago's .261. Six of the nine grade FAVORABLE. Butler is the only real bat (36% hard swings, covers soft stuff — velocity plan), and Bolte at the top swings max-effort on two of every three hacks — the highest rate we've ever graded — so he's a strikeout from ahead and the one who can change an inning from behind.

Everyone else is a plan, not a problem. Gelof's swing is the steepest on the card (+16°) and the curve is built for it. Clarke strikes out 37% of the time, Muncy 31%. Heim and Walton are steep, slow swings the changeup goes under. McNeil and Williams are slap-contact — two pitches and a ground ball. Strike one and this lineup runs out of answers fast.

Which is exactly why it matters: the finishing pitches have gone quiet. The changeup has zero swing-and-misses in two starts, the sweeper's miss rate is down to 18%, and the curve — still at 44% — came out four times in Atlanta. The movement is fine; it's height and usage. This is the card to bury the changeup, throw the curve, and let the count control you've rebuilt turn into strikeouts.

THE SHORT VERSION

The way we see it, this is the softest lineup you'll see all year — six of nine grade in your favor, and their whole core is on the IL. Butler is the one real bat, and he covers soft stuff, so he gets velocity. Bolte swings out of his shoes two out of every three times — from ahead he's a strikeout, from behind he's the one guy who can change an inning. Everybody else is a two-pitch ground ball or a strikeout hunt.

Which makes this the right start for the thing we talked about after Atlanta: the count control is back — now the finishing pitches need to come out. The changeup hasn't gotten a swing-and-miss in two starts because half of them are at the thigh; the curve came out four times against a lineup it could've finished. This card has four steep swings the curve is built for — Gelof, Heim, Serven, Walton. We'd love to see it featured, not saved.

So: strike one to everybody, changeup below the knees, curve early and often, sweeper away. The movement's home, the four-seam above the zone keeps finishing people, and the two hitters who matter only see pitches from ahead. That's the whole plan.
