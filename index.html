The Read

A different Red Sox team than July — and a different you.

Only three of the nine hitters from the Fenway game are still here — Abreu, Duran and Durbin — and two of them carry that night's lessons by name: Abreu struck out twice on buried changeups; Duran hit a sweeper and a changeup 104 mph, both at the thigh. The rest of the card is new: Rutschman is their catcher (a contact switch-hitter — zone him), Willson Contreras is at first (59% hard swings on the longest swing on the card), Roman Anthony leads off and is the one real bat — .296, half his swings max-effort, and a 33% miss rate against your mix that makes him a strikeout from ahead.

Around them: Jones and Sogard are steep swings built for the curve. Story strikes out 28% of the time with no damage. Durbin is slap contact. Five of the nine are lefty-side bats and four of those are changeup at-bats — the pitch that got 7 swing-and-misses Tuesday. Duran is the exception in that group: his flat swing covers soft stuff, and July proved it. He gets velocity.

The version of you showing up Sunday is the one from Tuesday: 76% first-pitch strikes, 13 of 17 to two strikes, zero walks, the changeup back below the knees. That plan against this card — Anthony and Contreras only from ahead, the lefties buried, Duran and Rutschman with hard stuff — is the whole game.

THE SHORT VERSION

The way we see it, this isn't the Boston team from July — six of the nine are new, and the three who aren't have the July lessons written on their cards. Abreu struck out twice on buried changeups; run it back. Duran hit two balls 104 on thigh-high soft stuff; his swing is flat, so he gets velocity, not the changeup. That one distinction is the difference between Fenway and Sunday.

Anthony is the bat. He swings out of his shoes half the time and misses a third of the time — from ahead he's a strikeout, from behind he's the one who changes the game. Contreras is the same profile from the right side, on the longest swing on the card — sweeper away from ahead. Everybody else is either a strikeout hunt (Story, Jones, Sogard) or a two-pitch out (Rutschman, Durbin).

And the good news carries: Tuesday was the full plan — strike one, changeup below the knees, seven strikeouts, no walks. The one thing we'd add is the curve. It's been thrown thirteen times in three starts, and this card has three swings it's built for. Same Tuesday, plus the curve.
