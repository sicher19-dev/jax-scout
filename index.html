The Read

Eight left-handed bats again. This time they don't miss.

Cleveland's card against righties — the one they ran in Game 5 to close out Chicago, and the same nine twice in September against righties, with Martínez and Genao the only swaps — is eight lefties around one righty, the Yankees' shape. The difference is contact. New York missed 28% of the time against your mix; this nine misses 20%, and the top of the order misses almost nothing: Kwan 6%, DeLauter 11%, Ramírez 15%. The lineup grades .273 against you — tougher than the Yankees (.256), a little under Atlanta and Philadelphia. So the changeup that's been your best pitch for a month is two different pitches here: a strikeout pitch to the bottom of the order (Bazzana, Bailey, Rocchio and Adell all miss it a third of the time) and a ground-ball pitch to the top, where it has to be below the knees or it's a hit.

Two bats decide it, and they're opposite problems. Chase DeLauter is the toughest grade on any card since Soto — .349 here, .353 in Cleveland — with 46% hard contact and an 11% miss rate: corners only, from ahead, and the walk beats the mistake. Steven Kwan is a 61-mph bat with 11% hard contact who walks 10% and misses 6%: throw him strikes, let him hit his single, and don't give him the walk he's waiting for. Ramírez is the name — two homers, a 106-mph double and a triple this week — and the number says LEAN (.286): your changeup struck him out twice when you were a Twin. In on the hands, soft stuff down, nothing middle. Adell is the strikeout at-bat and the one righty: sweeper away, changeup down, never the middle (108 off a sweeper over the plate in June), and make him swing — he walks 6%.

Two things carry from last week. You don't know your game or your role yet — Games 1, 2, 6 and 7 are the Trop, 3, 4 and 5 are Cleveland, and the rules on this card are written to work in both buildings and whether it's a start or an inning. And the roster is a check, not an assumption: Manzardo and Hoskins came back 9/28 and were left off the ALDS roster; the ALCS roster posts before Game 1. Both are graded in the roster box in case.
