The Read

Two bats that can end an inning, four that can't — and a park that punishes the one mistake.

This is the toughest lineup you've seen since coming back (.285), and it splits cleanly in two. Schwarber and Harper are the game. Schwarber swings max-effort on 69% of his hacks — more than any hitter we've graded for you this year — and still misses 40% of the time against the sweeper and changeup: a strikeout from ahead, a souvenir from behind. Harper is the toughest out on the card (.334): no hole in the swing, takes his walks, hits it hard. Both get the plan that handled Soto and Anthony — corners, count control, and the walk beats the mistake.

Then there's the other half. Arraez, Stott, Bohm and Crawford are contact hitters — Arraez has the slowest bat we've graded (61 mph), misses 5% of the time, and walks 4%. None of the four can be struck out on purpose and none of them hurt you when they connect. They get hard stuff in the zone and two-pitch outs. That's four of nine at-bats decided by efficiency, not stuff — and it's the difference between five innings and seven.

The strikeouts live with Realmuto (misses 40% on the changeup), Marsh, and Turner (sweeper away). And the whole thing runs on the two numbers from the last four starts: the changeup floor — 50% swing-and-miss over the last three starts when it lives below the knees — and strike one, which was a 7-strikeout night at 76% and a two-walk fifth at 58%.

The plan, by side

▸ vs Lefties (Schwarber · Harper · Arraez · Stott · Marsh · Crawford)

Two plans, not one. Schwarber and Marsh get the bury: sweeper off the plate, changeup at the shins, curve as the early look. Harper gets corners and patience. Arraez, Stott and Crawford get hard stuff in the zone — sinker for the ground ball, four-seam, two pitches and out. No changeups to those three; they put it in play.

▸ vs Righties (Turner · Bohm · Realmuto)

Turner: sweeper away, changeup down, no free bases. Bohm: sinker down-and-away, ground balls — his flat swing covers soft stuff. Realmuto is the strikeout at-bat: curve early under the steep swing, changeup down to finish, sweeper away.

THE SHORT VERSION

The way we see it, the last one of the year is a fitting one: the toughest lineup since you came back, in a park that punishes the one mistake, and it comes down to the two things the last four starts taught. Changeup below the knees, strike one to everybody. The ledger on the Carryover tab is the whole case — when those two numbers are up, it's seven strikeouts and no walks; when they're down, it's two walks and a 27-pitch inning.

Schwarber and Harper are the game. Schwarber swings out of his shoes more than anyone we've graded for you, and he still misses 40% against the sweeper and changeup — from ahead he's a strikeout, from behind he's a homer in this park. Harper has no hole; he gets the Soto plan — corners, and the walk beats the mistake. The other half of the lineup can't hurt you: Arraez, Stott, Bohm and Crawford put the ball in play softly, so they get hard stuff and two pitches. That's four at-bats where efficiency is the whole plan.

One honest change: we've asked for the curve three starts running, and it's been thrown eleven times with no swing-and-misses. So its job changes — an early look to the steep swings, and the changeup does the finishing. Everything else, you've already proven. Go close it out.
