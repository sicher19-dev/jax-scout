The Read

Eight left-handed bats. This is the changeup's series.

The Yankees' card against righties — the one they ran out tonight in the Wild Card — is eight lefties around one rookie righty, the most left-handed lineup you've faced all year. Your changeup is at 65% swing-and-miss over the last three starts and 44% at two strikes to lefties for the season; the surprise sinker has lefties 1-for-22 swinging; the four-seam at the letters struck out Bellinger in July. You've already done this to most of them: July 6, ten strikeouts, one hit, and six of these nine were in that lineup.

Two bats decide it. Ben Rice is their best lefty — .302 here, .317 in the Bronx — a 12% walker with .356 damage and a swing that still misses the curve a third of the time; he gets corners and count control. Spencer Jones is the strikeout at-bat and the danger at once: strikes out 35% of the time, misses 47% on the changeup, and swings max-effort on two of every three hacks. From ahead he's a strikeout; from behind he's a homer. García is the contact-with-pop exception — hard stuff and ground balls, no changeup diet. Everyone else is a plan you've already run.

Two things are different from a regular-season card. You don't know the park yet — Games 1, 2 and 5 are the Trop, 3 and 4 are the Bronx, where the right-field porch adds about fifteen points to every lefty's grade, and the rule "nothing up to the lefties" stops being advice and becomes the whole game. And the roster is a check, not an assumption: Judge is on the injured list and off the Wild Card roster; Stanton was activated yesterday after five months out. The ALDS roster posts before Game 1.

THE SHORT VERSION

The way we see it: this is the lineup your changeup was built for, and you've already shown them. Eight left-handed bats, and five of them struck out against you in July — Rice twice, Bellinger twice, McMahon twice, Chisholm, Wells. The changeup is at 65% misses over your last three starts and this is the card to use it on: below the knees to Jones, Wells, Chisholm and Rice, with the four-seam finishing above the zone and the surprise sinker once to each of the eight.

Two bats decide it. Rice gets corners and the count — he walks, and that's fine. Jones strikes out a third of the time and swings out of his shoes two of every three hacks: from ahead he's a strikeout, from behind he's a homer. The one thing to never do to either of them is the behind-count sinker. García is the contact guy — hard stuff and ground balls, no changeups.

And the two things that are different this week: you don't know the park yet, and you don't know the roster yet. If your start is Game 3 or 4, the porch in right turns "nothing up to a lefty" into the whole game — the rules on this card are written to work in both buildings. And the ALDS roster posts before Game 1: Judge is on the injured list, Stanton came off it yesterday; we'll check both the morning it's out. Same plan as July. Same plan as the last three weeks.
